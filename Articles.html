A Field Guide to Missing Data

I wrote a two-part guide on what economists do when the data they need aren't there, organised around 22 recurring problems: unobserved confounders, proxies, attrition, weak instruments, records that refuse to be linked, censored values, spillovers, revisions, and more. Each entry names the missing object, walks through the standard exercises for it, and says what stays unresolved afterwards. Master's Dissertation

You can download my Master's dissertation here. I wrote about how the Global Financial Crisis changed which subjects Leaving Cert candidates chose - the Leaving Cert being the Irish national entrance exam to third-level education. I found a wide gender gap in STEM subjects, and participation moved in different directions depending on the subject: more students took Agricultural Science, Applied Mathematics, Biology and Chemistry, while fewer took Engineering, Mathematics, Physics, and Physics and Chemistry. Admission points for STEM-related college courses also rose, especially in Healthcare, Chemistry, Nanotechnology, Biology and Computer Science.

Applying Kuhn's Concept of Scientific Revolutions to the History of Wave-Particle Duality

In this essay I follow the long argument over the nature of light and use it to make a case for studying philosophy and the history of science side by side. I lean on Kuhn's account of how communities of scientists organise themselves, and I ask whether the changes in the concept of light over the centuries can be classified as scientific revolutions - my answer is a qualified yes, since the period holds a couple of paradigm shifts, from Newton to Huygens and from Planck to Einstein to Bohm. Kuhn asked whether it is any wonder that the price of significant scientific advance is a commitment that runs the risk of being wrong, and I answer no: scientists spent three centuries unsure of what light is, the study of it still gave us technology we can't live without, and if humanity today still doesn't know what light is, then, well, does it bother?
